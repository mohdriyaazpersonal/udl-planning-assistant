# One goal, purposeful choices

## Lesson 1: How animal cells work

Invented grade 8 biology lesson; 24 learners; 45 minutes. Prepared 7 October 2026 with AI assistance. This is a planning demonstration, not a report of classroom use.

**Goal:** Explain how the cell membrane, nucleus and mitochondria each support an animal cell's functions, and predict one consequence if one structure stops functioning.

**Fixed evidence:** Every learner independently explains all three structure–function relationships and justifies one consequence through a causal chain. Scientific reasoning is assessed; spelling, drawing quality, oral fluency and unaided memory are not.

**Original design:** A dense diagram appears for three minutes, followed by vocabulary-heavy explanation, label copying and a paragraph from memory. The barrier scan identified transient information, passive participation, unexplained vocabulary, limited causal modeling, a blank-page planning demand and a single output format. These are hypotheses about the design, not diagnoses or observations of learners.

**Constraints:** Print and board first, grayscale-compatible, no paid tools or reliable internet. Provide readable text for essential visual information. Devices are optional. A short factual reference is permitted, but no completed answer to the assessed consequence question.

## Final starter set and timing

Use E1, E2, R1, R2, A1 and the two paper routes of A2 as one integrated lesson; they are small components rather than six separate activities. Defer recording until devices, quiet space and review time are established.

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Preview goal, evidence and rubric; silent initial prediction. |
| 5–13 | Read/revisit the cell reference and diagram; explain vocabulary. |
| 13–21 | Model the water-filter causal chain, then guided cell reasoning practice. |
| 21–25 | Optional solo or pair rehearsal; everyone notes their own reasoning. |
| 25–40 | Independent paragraph or map-with-sentences response, with optional blank organizer. |
| 40–45 | Self-check against the common criteria; collect responses and one barrier-feedback note. |

## Shared assessment criteria

Score each of the three structure–function explanations 0–2: 0 = absent/incorrect; 1 = a correct role named without explaining how it supports the cell; 2 = accurate role and an explicit link to the cell's functioning. Score the consequence chain 0–2: 0 = absent/incorrect; 1 = plausible consequence with a missing causal link; 2 = failure → disrupted function → plausible consequence with a clear causal explanation. Total: 8 points. Evidence target: 2 in each criterion; use criterion feedback rather than letting a high total conceal a missing structure.

The same rubric applies to every response mode. Do not score spelling, handwriting, artistic quality or performance confidence. A labeled diagram alone cannot earn full credit.

## Printable learner reference

**Cell membrane:** A thin boundary around the cell that selectively controls movement of substances into and out of it. It helps maintain the cell's internal conditions.

**Nucleus:** A compartment containing most of an animal cell's DNA. DNA carries instructions used in producing proteins that support cell activities.

**Mitochondria:** Structures in which many reactions of aerobic respiration occur, transferring energy from nutrients into ATP that the cell can use. They do not create energy from nothing, and they are not the only place ATP can be produced.

**Diagram text alternative:** A boundary encloses the animal cell's contents. Within it are a nucleus containing DNA and several mitochondria. Their locations in this simple schematic are illustrative; shapes, scale and arrangement vary between real cells.

Vocabulary: **selective** = permitting some things more readily than others; **DNA** = hereditary information; **protein** = a molecule with many structural and working roles in cells; **ATP** = a molecule used to transfer energy for cellular work; **consequence** = a result explained by a cause.

## Student task and blank organizer

For each of the three structures, explain its normal role and how that role supports the cell. Then select one structure, imagine its function is disrupted, and explain one plausible consequence. Choose a paragraph or a labeled map with explanatory sentences. Your format must show the same scientific reasoning.

| Structure | Normal role | How that role supports cell functioning |
|---|---|---|
| Cell membrane | __________________ | __________________ |
| Nucleus | __________________ | __________________ |
| Mitochondria | __________________ | __________________ |

My selected structure: ______. If its function is disrupted, ______ changes. This affects the cell because ______. Therefore, a plausible consequence is ______.

**Self-check:** Did I explain all three relationships? Is my consequence plausible? Did I connect cause and effect rather than only list words? Can another person follow my reasoning?
