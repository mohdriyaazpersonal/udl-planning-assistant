Used separate chats for a biology lesson, a workplace writing lesson, and stress tests. The transcripts preserve the generated answers and corrections; they do not represent a classroom trial or a conversation personally conducted by the user.

After a full reload, the saved instructions matched the 1,351-character mission prompt exactly. The fresh reuse transcript applies all three lenses and the correct CLEAR checks, keeps the final written evidence fixed, rejects both spoken-only substitution and copying, and ends with two refinement questions.

## Changes made during review

- Kept all three biology explanations and the causal prediction fixed across formats.
- Allowed a factual reference because memory was not the target; withheld completed assessed consequence chains.
- Added a silent solo rehearsal route and guaranteed paper output to avoid mandatory speaking or devices.
- Deferred recording until device access, quiet space and review time are established.
- Required explanatory sentences with a map; labels and presentation polish do not establish scientific reasoning.
- Used a water-filter reasoning example so the model does not give away the assessed cell answer.
- Applied the exact mission CLEAR definitions after catching terminology drift.

## Second-lesson comparison

The biology construct is scientific explanation, so paragraph and map-with-sentences responses can supply equivalent evidence. The workplace construct explicitly includes writing a coherent professional email, so a spoken-only response cannot supply equivalent evidence. In both lessons the final work remains individual, supports do not provide a completed answer, and a common rubric applies to available choices.

For the workplace starter set, select the readable fictional policy card, private scratch rehearsal and blank three-part planning strip. Keep all four policy constraints: check status first; replacement only after confirmed loss; refunds require supervisor approval; no delivery-date promise without verification. Score the actual email for acknowledgement, accurate policy application, an allowed next step, and coherent professional organization. Do not score a checked checklist as proof of competence.

## Limits and next improvement

The original in-chat prompts did not spell out every CLEAR definition, and the initial project-instruction save had not persisted. The resulting terminology drift was corrected. The final project uses the prescribed base instructions, and the added lesson-input-template.txt supplies the exact five definitions and questions for future runs. The assistant did not consistently end its initial responses with two refinement questions; the corrective prompt restored that requirement. These are observed limitations, not reasons to claim the tool is fully reliable.

No individual learner information was used. Neither lesson has been delivered to a class. Before reuse, an educator should review content accuracy, test material legibility and access in context, and assess whether the proposed timing and response modes are workable. A useful next addition would be a reusable input template containing the exact CLEAR questions, followed by checks across additional subjects.
